September was a record month only because of one sale
Observation
Cash collected reached ₹3,206,431 — the second-highest month in thirteen, and 27.4% above September 2025. Transactions fell to 272, the lowest count in the series.
Evidence
A single transaction (Sale ID 325708785, 14 Sep, 10 × Studio Private Class X 10 at ₹42,000) contributed ₹420,000 — 13.1% of the month. Remove it and September is ₹2,786,431: −7.0% against August and +10.7% against September 2025. Meanwhile attendance fell from 2,593 to 2,090 (−19.4%) and the renewal rate fell to 59.8%, its worst in twelve months.
Interpretation
The headline is a composition effect, not growth. Fewer people bought more expensive things. Average value per sale rose 50.2% MoM (₹7,846 → ₹11,788) purely because the mix shifted from ₹1,942 drop-ins toward annual memberships and private packages.
Why it matters
If September is reported as a record month, October will be planned against a base that includes a one-off ₹420,000 private sale that has not recurred in the prior twelve months. The plan would be built on ₹420,000 of revenue that does not exist.
Action
Report September two ways, every month. Publish "Total collected" and "Collected excluding single sales above ₹200,000" side by side. Set the October plan off the adjusted figure (₹2.79m), not the headline (₹3.21m).
Expected outcome
October planned against a realistic base; no false shortfall created by an artefact.
Success metric
Adjusted monthly revenue tracked as the primary series; variance to plan ≤ 5% in Q4.

The five numbers that should drive October

  • ₹2,786,431 — September revenue excluding the one-off private sale. This is the run-rate, not ₹3.21m.
  • 272 transactions — the lowest in thirteen months, down 22.3% year on year. Fewer people are buying.
  • 59.8% — the September paid-membership renewal rate, down from 75.7% in October 2025. The base is eroding while ticket size grows.
  • ₹1,534 — revenue per check-in, up 32.7% MoM. The studio is earning more from each visit and seeing fewer visits.
  • ₹1,304,458 — cash collected above revenue recognised (₹3.21m vs ₹1.90m). Deferred revenue is building; that liability is future attendance that has already been paid for.

Recognised revenue is the value of sessions actually consumed in the month (from the session ledger). Cash collected is what was invoiced. The gap is deferred revenue — membership value sold but not yet attended.

Revenue is now decoupled from attendance — and that is a warning, not a win
Observation
Over the last eight months cash collected per check-in has risen from ₹1,001 (Feb 2026) to ₹1,534 (Sep 2026), a 53% increase, while monthly check-ins fell from 2,593 to 2,090.
Evidence
Cash per check-in by month: Feb ₹1,001 · Mar ₹884 · Apr ₹2,310 · May ₹1,053 · Jun ₹956 · Jul ₹1,061 · Aug ₹1,156 · Sep ₹1,534. Recognised revenue per check-in moved far less, from ₹744 (Feb) to ₹910 (Sep), +22%.
Interpretation
Two separate things are happening. First, price realisation genuinely improved — members are paying more per visit than they did in February. Second, and more concerning, cash is arriving for visits that have not happened yet. The gap between cash and recognised revenue widened to ₹1,304,458 in September, the largest in the series.
Why it matters
Rising cash per check-in looks like pricing power. Much of it is deferred revenue — money banked against future attendance. If that attendance never materialises, the studio has sold capacity it must now deliver, into a schedule that is already running at 45.4% utilisation with falling footfall.
Action
Institute a monthly deferred-revenue roll-forward. Track opening balance, new sales, recognised consumption, and closing balance by product line. Report consumption rate (sessions used ÷ sessions sold in the trailing 90 days) alongside revenue.
Expected outcome
Visibility on whether September sales convert into October–December attendance or expire unused.
Success metric
Deferred revenue balance and its 90-day consumption rate reported monthly; consumption rate ≥ 70%.

Privates have become a top-three line on the back of two clients
Observation
Privates and virtual privates generated ₹621,317 in September — 19.4% of revenue, up from ₹26,000 (0.9%) in August, a 23-fold increase.
Evidence
Two clients account for the entire movement. Sale ID 325708785 (14 Sep, 10 × Private Class X 10, ₹420,000) and two Virtual Privates – Anisha x 10 packages (₹113,400). The remaining 17 private line items total ₹87,917. August had 7 private line items totalling ₹26,000; July ₹82,666 across 11.
Interpretation
This is not a trend, it is two transactions. Private revenue across the prior twelve months averaged ₹172,000 a month with a median of ₹48,700 — the September figure is 3.6× the trailing mean.
Why it matters
Privates are the highest-margin and most capacity-efficient line the studio sells: no class seat consumed, no schedule slot. They are also the least predictable. Any forecast that assumes September private revenue repeats will be wrong by roughly ₹420,000.
Action
Do not budget privates off September. Use the trailing-twelve median (₹48,700/month) as the baseline and treat anything above ₹150,000 as upside. Separately, run a deliberate private-sales push: 179 buyers in September, 2 bought privates. Attach rate is 1.1%.
Expected outcome
Stable private-revenue planning line and an explicit, measured attach-rate programme.
Success metric
Private attach rate (buyers purchasing any private product) rising from 1.1% to 4% within two quarters; monthly private revenue variance to plan ≤ 25%.

The median member is worth ₹481 a visit, not ₹1,128 — the unlimited model is being under-consumed
Observation
September clients attended 4.41 times each. At that frequency an annual member costs the studio ₹3,310 in delivered value against ₹175,560 collected — a per-visit realisation of ₹3,183, but the member is consuming only 1.02 visits a week against the three-a-week the product is priced for.
Evidence
474 distinct clients made 2,090 visits in September (4.41 each, 1.02 per week). Recognised revenue per check-in was ₹910. Attendance mix: memberships 1,376 check-ins (65.8%), packages 386 (18.5%), complimentary 191 (9.1%), single classes 115 (5.5%), intro offers 7 (0.3%), other 15 (0.7%).
Interpretation
Unlimited members are paying for three visits a week and using one. That is commercially excellent in the short run — it is why cash per check-in rose 53% since February — but it is precisely the condition that precedes non-renewal. The churn analysis in Report 07 shows members completing 11 or more sessions renew at 87.0% against 50.9% for those completing one to four.
Why it matters
Every rupee of the September margin improvement comes from members not using what they bought. That is not a durable revenue model; it converts into lapsed renewals one to three months later.
Action
Target consumption, not sales. Set a 30-day activation target of 6 visits for every new unlimited member and 3 for package holders. Assign the 63 active memberships expiring within 14 days to a named associate with a usage review before the renewal conversation.
Expected outcome
Higher consumption lifts renewal probability; the revenue already collected becomes earned rather than deferred-and-lapsed.
Success metric
Median visits per active member per month rising from 4.41 to 5.2; renewal rate recovering from 59.8% to 68% within two quarters.

Retail attaches to 14.5% of buyers and is effectively unmanaged
Observation
Retail generated ₹68,070 across 39 transactions from 26 of 179 buyers — an attach rate of 14.5% and ₹1,745 per retail transaction.
Evidence
Retail items are bars, drinks and small-format nutrition (SQ Raw Granola Bar, Cacao & Maca Ball, Impact Water 350ML). Average ₹1,745 per transaction is high for the category, indicating multi-item baskets rather than single impulse buys. September retail is 2.1% of revenue against 3.4% in October 2025 and 3.1% in December 2025.
Interpretation
Retail is holding roughly flat in absolute terms (₹65,646 in Oct 2025, ₹68,070 in Sep 2026) while total revenue grew. Its share has fallen by more than a third. This is a line that costs nothing to add and requires no schedule capacity.
Why it matters
At the observed ₹1,745 basket, moving attach from 14.5% to 30% of buyers adds roughly 28 baskets a month — about ₹48,900, or 1.5% of revenue, at zero marginal capacity cost.
Action
Make retail a scripted part of the check-in, not an afterthought. Place the basket at the desk, require the front desk to offer water with every check-in, and add a ₹1,500 retail threshold to the associate scorecard.
Expected outcome
Retail revenue rising from ~₹68,000 to ~₹110,000 a month within a quarter.
Success metric
Retail attach rate (retail buyers ÷ all buyers) from 14.5% to 30%; retail revenue ≥ 3% of total.

If the studio ranks sellers on revenue, it will conclude Tahira is underperforming by 43% when she is in fact handling nearly half the transaction volume. Ranking on revenue alone will push the team away from the small transactions that keep frequency high.
Action
Report sellers on revenue and on transactions side by side, and equalise access to high-ticket leads. Route every enquiry that mentions annual, private or multi-month to a rotating queue rather than to a named individual. Give each seller a monthly high-ticket target (≥ ₹50,000 items) alongside their volume target.
Expected outcome
More even distribution of high-value selling opportunities; a fairer and more diagnostic scorecard.
Success metric
Spread between highest and lowest average ticket narrowing from 1.83× to below 1.4× within two quarters, without any seller losing total revenue.

09

Cash Collected vs Revenue Earned

The studio collected ₹3.21m in September and delivered ₹1.90m of sessions. The ₹1.30m difference is a promise the schedule has to keep.

What the two numbers mean

  • ₹3,206,431 — cash invoiced in September (Sales file). This is money in the door.
  • ₹1,901,973 — value of sessions actually attended in September (session ledger). This is revenue earned.
  • ₹1,304,458 — the net increase in deferred revenue. Members bought entitlements they have not yet used.
  • 2,090 check-ins were delivered against 4,606 seats scheduled. There is 2,516 seats of unused capacity in the month to absorb future consumption.

Utilisation headroom

45.4% utilisation. Even if every entitlement sold in September were redeemed within 30 days, the September schedule could absorb it. The constraint on converting deferred revenue into earned revenue is not capacity — it is member behaviour.

Deferred revenue is rising faster than the studio can see
Observation
The gap between cash collected and revenue recognised has widened in six of the last twelve months and reached ₹1,304,458 in September, the largest in the series.
Evidence
Monthly gap (cash − recognised): Oct-25 ₹1,356,065 · Nov-25 ₹292,233 · Dec-25 ₹1,100,698 · Jan-26 ₹1,211,292 · Feb-26 ₹492,956 · Mar-26 ₹333,726 · Apr-26 ₹3,373,001 · May-26 ₹642,958 · Jun-26 ₹469,589 · Jul-26 ₹643,937 · Aug-26 ₹731,260 · Sep-26 ₹1,304,458.
Interpretation
Cumulatively the studio has banked substantially more entitlement than members are consuming. April 2026 alone added ₹3.37m of deferred revenue. Consumption is running at roughly 60% of sales value per month.
Why it matters
Deferred revenue is not profit. It is an obligation to deliver a service. If members lapse before consuming — and the September renewal rate of 59.8% says 40% of them do — the studio keeps the cash but loses the relationship and the future. Conversely, if consumption accelerates, the 45.4% utilisation schedule must absorb it.
Action
Build a monthly deferred-revenue schedule by product line with an expiry profile. Flag entitlements expiring within 30 days and contact those members with a use-it-or-renew prompt. Report the balance to the owner monthly.
Expected outcome
Deliberate management of the largest untracked liability in the business.
Success metric
Deferred-revenue balance tracked monthly with a 30-day expiry report; consumption rate (trailing-90-day sessions used ÷ sessions sold) reported and held above 70%.

Hidden Patterns

Six things the monthly total does not show.

1. The median transaction is ₹1,942, the mean is ₹11,788 — a 6.1× gap

114 of 272 line items (41.9%) were single sessions or format drop-ins priced at ₹1,942.50. They produced ₹225,880 — 7.0% of revenue. The mean is pulled up by 4 annuals, 11 private packages and 41 class packages. Any target expressed as "average transaction value" will be dominated by the four or five largest sales of the month and will move for reasons that have nothing to do with front-desk performance. Track the median and the count of items above ₹50,000 instead.

2. Money credits are being loaded, not spent — ₹6,226 across 21 transactions

21 line items loaded money credits at an average of ₹296. Money credits are stored value: they are a liability, not revenue, until redeemed. The loaded amount is trivial in September, but the mechanism matters because it is the only prepayment product in the file that does not expire with a membership. Money-credit balances are not tracked in any source provided and should be reported separately from revenue.

3. Point of sale takes 93.9% of revenue but the digital channels have a 2.1× lower ticket

POS: 239 items, ₹3,010,553, ₹12,575 avg. Checkout pages: 18 items, ₹126,814, ₹7,045 avg. Mobile app: 15 items, ₹69,064, ₹4,604 avg. Self-serve buyers buy drop-ins; the desk sells commitments. The digital channel is not a lower-cost substitute for the desk — it is a different, smaller-ticket product mix. Any plan to shift sales online without changing what is offered there will reduce revenue per transaction.

4. September has two clear campaign spikes and 27 quiet days

Day 1 (₹783,766, 24.4%) and day 14 (₹515,366, 16.1%) together are 40.5% of the month. The remaining 27 days averaged ₹70,700 each. September was two campaigns and a base business of roughly ₹1.9m. The base business, not the ₹3.21m, is what October inherits.

5. Discounting is concentrated: two codes are 78.6% of all discount given

OTTWENTEE (₹167,200) and PR1V@TE20 (₹100,000) account for ₹267,200 of ₹340,365 total. The other seven codes cost ₹73,165 across 34 line items. Discount governance is a two-code problem. Approving those two codes deliberately would control four-fifths of total discount spend.

6. Cash is 31.6% of collections — ₹1,013,197 across 68 line items

This is a material cash-handling exposure and a reconciliation burden. It also matters for autorenew: the churn analysis in Report 07 finds only 30 of 208 active memberships have a card on file. A business taking a third of its money in cash cannot switch on recurring billing without first running a card-capture programme.

Methodology, Reconciliation & Limitations

Every figure above, traceable to source.

Sources and scope

  • Primary: Sales – Raw – Sales (23).csv — 25,044 rows, 49 columns, payments at line-item grain, 4 Jul 2023 to 10 Oct 2026. 10,330 rows for Kwality House, Kemps Corner.
  • Supporting: Day End Report session ledger (328 September sessions) for attendance, capacity and recognised revenue; Bookings sheet (2,549 de-duplicated September bookings) for attendance mix.
  • Period: 1–30 September 2026. Twelve-month context uses October 2025 to September 2026; thirteen-month charts add September 2025 for year-on-year.
  • Studio: Kwality House, Kemps Corner only. Filtered on Calculated Location, never on a name pattern.

Reconciliation

  • 3 rows removed from 10,330: 2 with payment status other than succeeded and 1 voided. 10,327 rows retained.
  • Discounting is measured on "Sale Item Unit Discount Value", not "Discount Value In Currency". The latter repeats the sale-level total on each line of a multi-line sale; summing it yields ₹1,265,715 for September against a true ₹340,365. The per-line column reconciles exactly to the sale-level total on every single-line sale and to the sale total when summed across the 10 lines of the one large private sale.
  • Nine category totals sum to ₹3,206,431, matching the file total exactly.
  • Attributed seller revenue (₹2,991,129) plus unattributed (₹215,302) equals the month total. 35 line items carry no seller.
  • Attendance: session ledger 2,090 check-ins; checkins file 2,090 with Checked In = TRUE; Bookings sheet 1,941 after de-duplication. The 149 gap is documented in Report 08 §3.

Definitions

  • Cash collected — sum of Payment Value, which is the net amount paid after discount. Verified: on every discounted line, Payment Value equals unit price minus unit discount.
  • List value — cash collected plus discount. Discount rate — discount ÷ list value.
  • Average value per sale — revenue ÷ distinct Sale ID (₹13,762). Per line item — revenue ÷ rows (₹11,788). Both are reported because the source is at line-item grain and a single sale may carry 10 rows.
  • Recognised revenue — Revenue on the session ledger, being the value of sessions consumed. A different measure from cash; the difference is deferred revenue.
  • Renewal rate — renewals ÷ (renewals + lapses) for paid memberships expiring in the month, excluding frozen. Source and full method in Report 07.

Limitations — the five that matter most

  • No cost data exists in any source. No instructor rates, rent, or product cost. Every margin statement in this report is inference from price and mix, not measured profitability. This is the largest gap.
  • Recognised revenue is supplied, not derived. The session ledger provides a Revenue figure per class with no stated allocation method. The deferred-revenue gap is therefore as reliable as that field, and should be confirmed against the studio's own accounting.
  • Discount attribution is imperfect. 35 line items carry no seller and 1 line item carries no discount code. Discount by seller cannot be computed reliably and is not reported.
  • October 2026 is partial (1–10 October only). It is used for the win-back window in Report 07 and nowhere as a benchmark.
  • Refunds are not netted. The file carries a refund flag but no refund amounts against September Kwality House line items; revenue here is gross of any subsequent refund.

A note on confidence labels

High confidence means the finding is directly measurable in the reconciled data with a stable sample. Moderate means the direction is well supported but the magnitude rests on an assumption stated in the text. Low means the finding depends on behaviour the data cannot observe; no such item is presented as fact anywhere in this report.
